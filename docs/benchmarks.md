## Method

- **Corpus:** Xiph.org derf CIF sequences (akiyo = low motion, foreman = medium, mobile = high), first 100 frames, 4:2:0. Audio is 30 s mono 48 kHz excerpts of the Sintel trailer audio and Elephants Dream's music (ED-CM stereo, downmixed).
- **Video metric:** every codec's output is decoded and scored by the *same* ffmpeg PSNR and SSIM filters against the source. Rate is the raw payload, with no container overhead on either side. Each codec is swept over its quality setting; a single point means little, the curve is the result.
- **Video reference ceiling:** no true rate-distortion bound exists for natural video, so the practical ceiling is the best of x264 `veryslow`, x265 `veryslow` and SVT-AV1 preset 4, at default tuning.
- **Video equal-quality column:** how many times *larger* the tpt stream is than the best reference at the same luma PSNR (log-linear interpolation). `>1` means tpt is worse; `n/a` means the PSNR is outside the reference's range.
- **Audio, lossless:** Aura is compared by size against FLAC and against an *estimate* of an ideal predictor (order-32 LPC per 4096-sample block, ideal Laplace entropy coder, side info ignored). It is an estimate, not a proven bound.
- **Audio, lossy:** Cinder is swept over bit depth (4–12, via `--quality`) and per-band step (`--band-step`, half-octave grid from 1/1024 to about 45). Cinder codes every coefficient at a fixed width with no entropy coding, so its rate depends only on the bit depth; the step is the only free choice at each rate. Rate is the codec's payload, not the file size. The Cinder rows therefore use the best step found for each rate, which is an oracle choice: a shipped encoder would need rate control to find it.
- **Audio, lossy reference:** Opus (libopus via ffmpeg, mono 48 kHz) at 12–128 kbps. Opus is perceptually tuned and SNR is a waveform metric, so Opus is expected to look worse here than it sounds; read the SNR columns as a waveform-fidelity comparison, not a listening test.
- **Audio, bound:** the Gaussian rate-distortion bound from reverse water-filling over 16 bands per 256-sample DCT block, each band's variance taken as its power in that block, plus 4 bits per band per block of side information (about 12 kbps). It is an estimate for that envelope model, not a proven bound on every source. Earlier runs used per-coefficient variances with no side information; those numbers were far too optimistic and are not comparable.
- **Cinder's wire step:** version 3 carries steps in 1/1024 units. Version 2 wrote whole numbers only, which made every step below 1.0 decode to near silence; the rows below post-date that fix.
- **Cinder's loudest peaks clip at fine steps.** At 12 bits the range is about ±2048 × step, so a step of 0.001 clips coefficients above about 2. The sweep includes those points; the SNR column shows the cost.
